I'm studying to make the transition from software engineering to AI
engineering. As part of this process, I'm following Ed Donner's series of
courses on Udemy.

What I'm enjoying most is how easy it is to follow the explanations,
understand the concepts, and put them into practice quickly. I've already
been able to apply some of what I've learned to a project you can try on
this very site: my digital twin.

## The courses I'm following

### Completed

- [AI Engineer Core Track: LLM Engineering, RAG, QLoRA, Agents](https://www.udemy.com/share/10bOXH/).

### In progress

- [AI Engineer Agentic Track: The Complete Agent & MCP Course](https://www.udemy.com/share/10dasB/).

### My next courses

## Learning by building: my digital twin

As part of my learning in these courses, I built a chatbot that can answer
questions about my career, skills, and professional experience.

The project combines OpenAI models with retrieval-augmented generation
(RAG), reranking, and query rephrasing. It has helped me turn the course
concepts into an application that other people can use.

You can try it using the floating chat button on the [homepage](/), or
access my [digital twin on Hugging Face Spaces](https://seba3c-digital-twin.hf.space)
directly.

## Why I recommend them

Based on my experience with the course I've completed and the one I'm
currently taking, I recommend this series to anyone who wants to get into
AI engineering. The explanations are easy to follow and understand, and
you can put what you learn into practice quickly.

I still have a lot to learn. For now, following these courses and building
my own projects has been a good way to work toward my goal of becoming an
AI Engineer.
